**Method:** Public-web research only (search + page fetches). No purchases, no accounts, no sign-ins. Three parallel researchers covered 5 names each.

## Method caveats (read before acting)

- Direct npm/PyPI registry fetches failed technically for most names this session; those cells say **"check failed"** — never read as clearance. Where search gave direct evidence (e.g., Socket listing, installable package), it is cited.
- Domain cells say **"no signal"** where search returned nothing — that is *not* confirmation of availability. Direct WHOIS/registrar checks are still needed for any finalist.
- Trademark signals come from web search (third-party reports, USPTO-indexed documents), not independent TESS verification. This is a **preliminary screen, not legal clearance**. Real trademark questions go to a lawyer (per standing practice).

## Ranked recommendation

### Top 3

1. **NerveGrid** (Medium) — the only name without a live company, active project, or trademark signal. Inactive hackathon repos are the only GitHub footprint; no npm/PyPI/package conflicts surfaced. Infrastructure feel fits ("central nervous system for an AI workforce") and it works with the planned positioning: *NerveGrid — Mission control for humans, agents, models, tools, and autonomous work.* **Before committing:** direct WHOIS on nervegrid.com/.io and a USPTO TESS status check, since domain cells returned "no signal" rather than confirmed availability.

2. **Operantix** (High, but least-bad of the High group) — the only conflict is OPERANTIX SYSTEMS PRIVATE LIMITED, an India-registered non-AI IT firm. No live AI product, no active AI repos, no trademark signal. Enterprise/technical feel. Risk is a same-name company in an adjacent-ish (IT services) field, but non-US and non-AI.

3. **Synvara** (High — include only because it's Antonio's lean, with a red flag) — **forge.synvara.ai is a live AI product** (browser-native IDE, Show HN Nov 2025) plus an exact-name GitHub org. The collision is in AI dev tools, not exactly our category (workforce control plane), but it is active, recent, and exact-name. Recommend against unless Antonio consciously accepts the collision risk. Do not build six months of identity on it without deeper diligence.

## Strategic note

14 of 15 screened names are High risk. That suggests the "invented but pronounceable" space around AI-workforce branding is heavily mined. Two paths:

- **A. Commit to NerveGrid** after WHOIS + TESS verification — cleanest screen, fits infrastructure-first positioning.
- **B. Generate a fresh batch** of more invented/compound names (the 5 unscreened from Antonio's original 20 — Operon AI, WorkforceX, SentinelMesh, Agentarium, Nexum — plus new inventions) and run the same screen. Note Antonio already flagged Operon as trademark-risky and WorkforceX as too generic.

Either way, do not lock the brand until a finalist clears a direct registry/domain check and (for the final pick) proper trademark review.
